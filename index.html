Water markets in Northern China

University of Waterloo, April 2026

Looked at why water rights trading in Northern China falls short, given a region with 10% of the country's fresh water but 30% of its population. Assessed three fixes for efficiency and equity, and recommended clearer water rights law, smart-meter monitoring and targeted incentives, with legal clarification ranked best on both.

The British Empire's Legacy in the Global Trade of Plastic Waste

University of Waterloo, March 2025

Traced how British entrepôts in Singapore, Malacca, Penang and Hong Kong, built to ship rubber and tin, became hubs of today's $1.2 trillion plastics trade. Drew on colonial archives, treaties and trade data to show how Malaysia absorbed the cost of the waste after China's 2018 import ban.

Integrated Assessment Model

University of Waterloo, April 2024

With two teammates, implemented a three-region (high, medium, low income) integrated assessment model using a set of provided equations. Ran scenarios on emission targets and discount rates out to 2300, and found that the social cost of carbon was 11 to 21 times higher at a 1% discount rate than at 5%, depending on the region.

Senior honours essay

Investigating the Impact of Bill 187 on Ontario's Electronics Industry

Senior honours essay, University of Waterloo, December 2025. Supervised by Dr. Predrag Rajsic.

Ontario's Bill 187 proposes a right to repair for electronics. This essay asks what it could mean for manufacturers, repair businesses and consumers. It uses Ontario industry and trade data to look at firms' cost structures, the size of the repair market, and possible effects on consumer behaviour and electronic waste. It then compares the bill with right-to-repair laws in the EU, UK and US to find design gaps that affect repair access and how long products last.

  • Conclusion: Bill 187 makes repair easier by opening up spare parts and repair information, but without rules on cost, planned obsolescence, and consumer trust, most people will likely keep replacing devices instead of repairing them.
  • Skills developed: Industry and trade data analysis, comparative policy analysis, literature review, data visualization
